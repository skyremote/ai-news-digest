# AI Digest — 2026-09-06

## TL;DR

OpenAI declared it will cut Cursor's API access by November, drawing a hard line between its model portfolio and Elon Musk's corporate orbit — the first time the company has terminated a major partner on security-of-use grounds rather than price. GPT-6 Astra finished its broad rollout to all paid ChatGPT tiers while two regional newsrooms joined the media industry's growing copyright litigation stack against AI training. Debian's vote to hold contributors accountable for AI-assisted code — rather than ban or bless it — is this week's durable policy signal, one that will propagate through downstream Linux distributions and influence corporate open-source governance for years.

## Top 5

1. **[Our decision on Cursor following its acquisition by SpaceX](https://openai.com/index/our-decision-on-cursor-following-its-acquisition-by-spacex/)** — *OpenAI* — OpenAI is winding down Cursor's model access by 12 November, citing inability to ensure compliance with its terms of service under SpaceX ownership; the implicit argument is that Musk-controlled entities are an untrusted party regardless of contract.

2. **[GPT-6 Astra rolls out to all paid ChatGPT tiers](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/03/open-ai-astra-gpt-6-cyber.html)** — *CNBC* — 1.05M-context window, $10/$50 per million tokens input/output, with a restricted variant that hard-declines certain cybersecurity prompts; the headline benchmark numbers beat GPT-5 across most evals, though independent replication is still thin.

3. **[Seattle Times and Newsday are the latest publications to sue OpenAI and Microsoft](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/05/seattle-times-and-newsday-are-the-latest-publications-to-sue-openai-and-microsoft/)** — *TechCrunch* — SDNY complaint alleges scraping of paywalled content for training; the publishers are seeking damages and deletion of training sets, adding regional weight to what is now a substantial body of coordinated media litigation.

4. **[Introducing WeatherNext 3, our most advanced and accurate global weather AI model](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/introducing-weathernext-3/)** — *Google DeepMind* — Hourly forecasts at 5 km resolution, up to 50% more accurate precipitation predictions, and live integration into Maps Platform, BigQuery, and Earth Engine; this is the first AI weather model that ingests real-time satellite data rather than waiting for NWP's six-hour analysis cycle.

5. **[Debian votes to allow "responsible use of generative AI"](https://www.debian.org/vote/2026/vote_002)** — *Debian Project* — Eight-option General Resolution settles on contributor accountability: AI-assisted code is neither banned nor exempt — the human who submits it owns the outcome; passed among Debian Developers who voted.

## Signal, not noise

**[General Resolution: LLM usage in Debian](https://www.debian.org/vote/2026/vote_002)** — *Debian Project*

The Debian vote is easy to file under "open-source community drama," but the model it chose — contributor-owns-the-output, no new rules beyond existing standards — is almost certainly what the rest of institutional software development will converge on. Debian is the base for Ubuntu, Raspberry Pi OS, Tails, and several hundred downstream distributions; the precedent will be imported wholesale rather than re-litigated. In three months, when corporate engineering teams and other distributions write their own AI contribution policies, this resolution is the document they will cite as prior art.
