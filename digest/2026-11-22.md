The BIS certification rule is not a chip ban — it is a supply-chain audit requirement baked permanently into the hardware distribution layer. Every data centre that buys Nvidia GPUs going forward must maintain records proving it is not serving Chinese customers, which means US-designed accelerators are now gated behind ongoing compliance infrastructure rather than a one-time export licence. Jefferies estimates the revenue drag at $44–62B for Nvidia in 2027 alone, but the second-order effect is bigger: it accelerates Huawei Ascend and domestic Chinese GPU adoption far faster than any export ban had managed, and forces hyperscalers in Malaysia, Singapore and the Gulf to pick a side. In three months, the industry will be mapping which clusters are "clean" and which are not — and the answer will reshape where foundation model training happens for the rest of the decade.
