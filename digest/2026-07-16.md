## TL;DR

China's AI Companion Act came into force on July 15, compelling ByteDance (Doubao) and Alibaba (Qwen) to shut off persistent humanlike agent features used by over 345 million people — the first large-scale compulsory product change driven by an AI regulation. Two arXiv papers published the same day mount a coordinated critique of how model capability is actually measured: aggregate accuracy masks systematic prediction flips caused by irrelevant contextual variation, and LLM-as-judge scoring inflates without a reference answer. Against that backdrop, Meta's custom inference chip Iris enters production in September, while TSMC's all-time June revenue record ($13.75B, +68% YoY) confirms that AI hardware demand is consuming every wafer the foundry can produce.

## Signal, not noise

**[The Illusion of Robustness: Aggregate Accuracy Hides Prediction Flips under Task-Irrelevant Context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12963)** — *arXiv, Zhang, Koyejo & Yang*

The finding that models can flip answers on the same question depending on irrelevant contextual wrapping is not a new intuition, but this paper's systematic characterisation of the phenomenon — and its demonstration that it persists even at high aggregate accuracy — is methodologically significant. In three months, expect it to inform how capability claims are qualified in technical reports and to surface in enterprise procurement as a demand for distributional robustness testing. Paired with the LLM-judge inflation paper from the same day, it draws a troubling picture: the scaffolding of evals-driven progress narratives is shakier than publicly acknowledged, and the labs that figure out how to measure real robustness first will have a durable credibility advantage.
