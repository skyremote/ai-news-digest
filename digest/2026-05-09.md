# AI News Digest — 2026-05-09

## Executive Summary

The Trump administration is drafting an executive order that would impose mandatory pre-release safety testing on frontier AI models — a full policy reversal triggered by Anthropic's unreleased Mythos, a model the company privately warned can autonomously exploit decades-old vulnerabilities in browsers and critical infrastructure. In the same breath, Anthropic is finalising a $50B funding round at a ~$900B valuation, underpinned by annualised revenue that grew from $9B to roughly $45B in six months. Cutting across both stories, a cross-vendor security analysis this week found that every documented exploit targeting Claude Code, Codex, GitHub Copilot, and Vertex AI ignored the model layer entirely and went straight for the OAuth credential — a systemic architectural gap that no model-level safety measure can close.

## Top 5 Stories

**1. White House pivots to FDA-style vetting for AI models**

The Trump administration — which previously opposed AI-specific regulation — is preparing an executive order that would require frontier AI models to pass safety evaluations before public release. National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett explicitly compared the process to FDA drug approval. The proximate cause is Anthropic's Mythos: a model Anthropic released only to a limited set of Wall Street banks, with a private warning that its autonomous vulnerability-discovery capabilities were not safe for broader deployment. Google, Microsoft, and xAI have agreed to provide early model access to the government; OpenAI and Anthropic were already part of a voluntary Commerce Department testing programme. *— [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-06/white-house-preps-order-to-boost-ai-security-hassett-says), [The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/5866292-white-house-ai-evaluation-process/), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/06/trump-administration-embraces-ai-oversight-policies-it-once-rejected-anthropic-mythos-caisi/)*

*Take: Anthropic built the model that made AI deregulation politically untenable — a move that hands safety-minded incumbents a structural advantage in any future licensing regime.*

**2. Anthropic closing in on $900B valuation**

A $50B funding round — the largest in private-company history — is expected to close within days, valuing Anthropic at $850B–$900B and eclipsing OpenAI's $852B post-money mark. The number is credible: annualised revenue grew from $9B at end-2025 to roughly $45B by April 2026, with Claude Code and Cowork cited as the primary drivers. A board decision on final terms and an IPO timeline (potentially October 2026) is expected at the next board meeting. *— [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/29/sources-anthropic-could-raise-a-new-50b-round-at-a-valuation-of-900b/), [the-decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-approaches-1-trillion-valuation-as-revenue-grows-fivefold/)*

*Take: Five-fold revenue growth in six months is the actual story; the valuation is just arithmetic applied to it.*

---

**3. AI coding-agent exploits: every attacker went for the credential**

VentureBeat's analysis of six separate exploits disclosed between 2025 and 2026 — targeting OpenAI Codex, Anthropic Claude Code, GitHub Copilot, and Google Vertex AI — found a single shared attack pattern: steal the OAuth token or service-account credential the agent holds, then walk through the front door of production. No attacker needed to jailbreak or subvert the model itself. In each case, the credential existed and was reachable before any validation layer could fire. A separate SecurityWeek report confirms that Claude Code, Gemini CLI, and GitHub Copilot Agent are all independently vulnerable to prompt injection via GitHub PR comments and issue bodies, providing a delivery mechanism for the same credential-theft playbook. *— [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/security/six-exploits-broke-ai-coding-agents-iam-never-saw-them), [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/claude-code-gemini-cli-github-copilot-agents-vulnerable-to-prompt-injection-via-comments/)*

*Take: The attack surface for agentic AI is IAM, not the model — which means the security team owns this problem more than the AI team does.*

---

**4. Five Eyes publish first joint agentic AI security guidance**

CISA, the NSA, and their counterparts in the UK (NCSC), Australia (ASD/ACSC), Canada (CCCS), and New Zealand (NCSC-NZ) released a 30-page joint document on April 30 titled "Careful Adoption of Agentic AI Services." It classifies agentic deployment risks into five categories — privilege, design and configuration, behavioral, structural, and accountability — and recommends zero-trust architecture, least-privilege access, and human oversight gates for high-consequence actions as baseline controls. *— [CISA/DoD PDF](https://media.defense.gov/2026/Apr/30/2003922823/-1/-1/0/CAREFUL%20ADOPTION%20OF%20AGENTIC%20AI%20SERVICES_FINAL.PDF), [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/cisa-nsa-five-eyes-guidance-secure-deployment-ai-agents/)*

*Take: The five risk categories map almost exactly onto the attack patterns in story 3 — that convergence means practitioners should read this document as a pre-compliance checklist, not a discussion paper.*

**5. JPMorgan Chase moves AI into its core-infrastructure budget**

The largest US bank's 2026 technology budget is $19.8B, of which roughly $2B is now explicitly tagged as AI — and the accounting category is "core infrastructure," not R&D or experimental. JPMorgan has 2,000 staff dedicated to AI across productivity tooling, cybersecurity hardening, and personalised retail banking. The reclassification is more significant than the dollar figure: it means AI failures will be treated as infrastructure failures, not innovation-programme misses. *— [Banking Exchange](https://www.bankingexchange.com/news-feed/item/10520-jp-morgan-chase-reclassifies-ai-spending-as-core-infrastructure), [AI News](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/jpmorgan-chase-treats-ai-spending-as-core-infrastructure/)*

*Take: When a bank's CIO reclassifies AI as infrastructure in the annual report, every other F500 CIO reads that slide deck.*

## Signal-Not-Noise

**The Five Eyes agentic AI guidance document (story 4) — not the White House EO (story 1).**

The executive order gets the headlines, but it is still being drafted. The CISA/Five-Eyes document is already published, already actionable, and already aligned with the real-world attack patterns documented in story 3. The "privilege risk" category it defines is essentially a formalization of the credential-theft attack surface that VentureBeat described independently — meaning two distinct communities (intelligence agencies and security researchers) converged on the same threat model without coordinating. When that happens, the threat model is almost certainly correct. Organisations deploying agentic AI should treat the 30-page PDF as a gap-analysis tool against their current IAM posture before any regulatory deadline forces them to.
