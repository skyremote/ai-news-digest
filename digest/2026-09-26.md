The three labs that control the overwhelming majority of frontier model capacity are writing the compliance rulebook for everyone deploying those models — and they are doing it before any legislature forces their hand. The FINRA analogy is apt: Wall Street hated self-regulation too, but once FINRA was stood up it became the unavoidable audit and licensing infrastructure for the whole industry. If Krishnan takes the role and this body reaches general availability by Q1 2027 as expected, every company shipping an AI-powered product will face incident-reporting obligations and auditor qualifications that were drafted by their primary suppliers. Whether that is good safety governance or regulatory moat-building, the practical result is the same: the compliance baseline is being set right now, and retrofitting to it later will cost far more than understanding it today.
