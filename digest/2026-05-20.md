# AI News Digest — 2026-05-20

## Executive Summary

Google used I/O to ship two models and a personal agent in a single keynote, signaling it has moved from catch-up to coherent strategy. On the same day, the UK's AI Security Institute published findings that Claude Mythos Preview can autonomously chain a 32-step enterprise attack end-to-end — the first frontier model to do so — forcing a rethink of what "AI risk" means in practice. Meanwhile, Anthropic disclosed it has passed OpenAI in annualized revenue, though a methodology dispute over cloud-partner gross recognition may revise that figure downward.

## Top 5 Stories

**1. Google I/O 2026: Spark, Omni, and Gemini 3.5 Flash**

Google shipped Gemini Spark — a personal agent that runs in Google Cloud VMs 24/7 and acts on your behalf across Gmail, Docs, and Calendar — alongside Gemini Omni, a world model that generates physics-aware video from any input modality, and Gemini 3.5 Flash, which Google calls its strongest agentic and coding model yet. The bet is vertical integration: the company that owns your data, your cloud, and your model collapsing the stack into a single product surface.

*— [Google I/O 2026 announcements](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/google-io-2026-collection/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/19/google-ai-ultra-gemini-spark-omni.html)*

**2. Claude Mythos becomes the first AI to complete a 32-step autonomous enterprise attack**

The UK AI Security Institute evaluated Anthropic's Claude Mythos Preview and found it completed a full enterprise network attack simulation end-to-end — something no prior frontier model achieved — while hitting 73% on expert-level capture-the-flag tasks. AISI is careful to note it has not tested well-defended production systems, but the autonomous offensive threshold has been crossed in a controlled lab setting.

*— [AISI evaluation](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/our-evaluation-of-claude-mythos-previews-cyber-capabilities)*

**3. Anthropic's ARR passes OpenAI's — with an asterisk**

Anthropic reported a $30B annualized run rate against OpenAI's $25B disclosed in February, driven by 8 of the Fortune 10 using Claude and Claude Code alone reaching $2.5B ARR in under a year. OpenAI's CRO disputed the figure, arguing Anthropic recognizes gross cloud-partner consumption rather than its net share — a roughly $8B discrepancy under more conservative accounting.

*— [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropic-says-it-hit-a-30-billion-revenue-run-rate-after-crazy-80x-growth)*

**4. NVIDIA ships GR00T N1.7 with the first empirical dexterity scaling law**

GR00T N1.7, now in commercial early access, was pre-trained on 20,854 hours of human egocentric video spanning 20-plus task categories and demonstrates the first documented scaling law for robot dexterity: more egocentric data produces predictable, consistent improvements in manipulation capability. NVIDIA also previewed GR00T N2, a world-action model architecture that generalizes to new tasks in new environments more than twice as often as current VLA baselines.

*— [NVIDIA Newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-accelerates-robotics-research-and-development-with-new-open-models-and-simulation-libraries), [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/gr00t-n1-7)*

**5. HN front page: "There Will Be a Scientific Theory of Deep Learning"**

An arXiv paper arguing the field is close to a unified predictive theory — one that would derive test-loss power laws from first principles, explain grokking, and generalize across architectures — is generating substantive HN discussion. The paper maps open empirical regularities to candidate formalisms: large deviation theory for grokking timescales, information geometry for preconditioners, quantum measurement theory for feature superposition. The argument is that these patterns are too consistent across settings to remain theoretically unaccounted for.

*— [arXiv 2604.21691](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21691v1), [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47893779)*

## Signal Not Noise

**The AISI's Mythos evaluation is the most consequential document published this week** — not because any single finding is shocking in isolation, but because it makes explicit what has been implicit in capability evals for two years: frontier models can now autonomously reason across multi-step adversarial chains without human scaffolding. The implication is not that Claude is dangerous to deploy; it's that "AI risk" needs to split into two distinct problems. Alignment risk asks whether the model does what you want. Autonomous capability risk asks what the model can do when pointed at an adversarial objective by anyone with API access. The AISI report is the first official government publication to demonstrate the second category concretely, on a production-released model. Policy frameworks that haven't modeled these as separate threat surfaces are now measurably behind.

*— [AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/our-evaluation-of-claude-mythos-previews-cyber-capabilities), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/new-claude-mythos-becomes-the-first-ai-model-to-clear-all-cyberattack-simulations-from-britains-ai-safety-agency/)*
