# AI News Digest — 2026-05-27

## Executive Summary

Anthropic's Mythos model, distributed under Project Glasswing to select critical-infrastructure partners, autonomously discovered tens of thousands of previously unknown vulnerabilities across every major OS and browser—outpacing decades of human security review and redefining what "attack surface" means at frontier capability levels. Google's I/O keynote last week unveiled Gemini 3.5 Flash and the Antigravity agent platform, consolidating its agentic developer stack, while Apple's forthcoming iOS 27 moves in the opposite direction, opening Siri and Apple Intelligence to Claude, Gemini, and third-party models via an "Extensions" framework. Behind the product layer, structural bets are hardening: Meta raised 2026 capex guidance to $145 billion, the EU agreed to delay high-risk AI compliance by 16 months, and a $650M self-improving-AI startup exited stealth.

## Top 5 Stories

**1. Anthropic's Mythos finds 300 Firefox zero-days—and tens of thousands more across critical software**

Anthropic previewed Claude Mythos through Project Glasswing, a controlled release to AWS, Apple, Cisco, Google, JPMorgan Chase, and Microsoft. The model fully autonomously identified and exploited a 17-year-old FreeBSD RCE (CVE-2026-4747), found nearly 300 vulnerabilities in Firefox where a prior Anthropic model had found roughly 20, and surfaced zero-days in every major browser. Total count across all software now runs into the tens of thousands. Anthropic is holding back a broad release while defenders patch—an asymmetry that will not hold indefinitely.

_Take: A model that outpaces decades of human security review is no longer theoretical; the velocity gap between AI-assisted discovery and human-paced remediation is now the central security risk of the decade._

*— Anthropic red team blog, [red.anthropic.com/2026/mythos-preview](https://red.anthropic.com/2026/mythos-preview/); [CNBC, 5 May 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/05/anthropic-ceo-cyber-moment-of-danger-mythos-vulnerabilities.html)*

**2. Apple opens iOS 27's AI layer to Gemini and Claude via "Extensions"**

Apple is building a framework called Extensions into iOS 27, iPadOS 27, and macOS 27 that lets users assign third-party models—Gemini, Claude, Grok—to power Siri, Writing Tools, and Image Playground. Siri will announce which model is responding via a distinct voice. Closed-door testing with Google and Anthropic is already underway; public preview is expected at WWDC this summer.

_Take: Apple's defensive moat around Siri just became a distribution channel for its rivals, giving Google and Anthropic first-party presence on over a billion devices._

*— [TechCrunch, 5 May 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/05/apple-plans-to-make-ios-27-a-choose-your-own-adventure-of-ai-models/); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-05/ios-27-features-apple-plans-to-let-users-swap-models-across-apple-intelligence)*

**3. Google I/O: Gemini 3.5 Flash goes GA; Antigravity becomes the unified agent runtime**

At its May 19 keynote, Google released Gemini 3.5 Flash—claiming parity with large flagship models at Flash-tier latency—and announced it is retiring competing orchestration paths in favour of Antigravity, its agent-first development platform. Gemini Intelligence lands at the Android OS layer to anticipate user intent across third-party apps. Google also introduced TPU 8i (inference-optimised for agents) and TPU 8t (training on unified memory) at Cloud Next '26.

_Take: Google is betting the developer ecosystem on a single agentic runtime rather than letting the framework landscape fragment—a platform play that mirrors what AWS did with Lambda in the serverless era._

*— [Google I/O 2026 keynote](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/sundar-pichai-io-2026/); [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/all-the-news-from-the-google-io-2026-developer-keynote/)*

**4. EU agrees to delay high-risk AI Act compliance by 16 months under Omnibus VII**

The Council and Parliament reached a provisional agreement on 7 May streamlining AI Act obligations. Annex III high-risk AI systems (use-based deployments) get a deadline extension from August 2026 to December 2027. SME exemptions now extend to small mid-caps. Two new outright prohibitions are added: AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM, effective December 2026.

_Take: The delay is a pragmatic concession to implementation reality, but enterprises with Annex III systems already in production now have less urgency to audit them—a gift that deferred compliance risk rather than eliminating it._

*— [European Council press release, 7 May 2026](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/05/07/artificial-intelligence-council-and-parliament-agree-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/)*

**5. Meta raises 2026 capex guidance to $145 billion**

Meta updated full-year 2026 capital expenditure guidance from $115–135B to $125–145B, up from $72B in 2025. The increase reflects higher component prices, additional data centre capacity, and the newly created Meta Compute division. A gigawatt-scale campus in Louisiana is being built via a $27B joint venture with Blue Owl. Shares fell more than 6% after-hours on the announcement.

_Take: The infrastructure spending race has moved past "expensive" into geopolitically significant—individual company data centre commitments now rival the grid capacity of small nations._

*— [Fortune, 29 Apr 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/04/29/meta-zuckerberg-145-billion-ai-spending-roi/); [Data Centre Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/meta-estimates-2026-capex-to-be-between-115-135bn/)*

## Signal-Not-Noise Pick

**Project Glasswing and the patch-velocity problem**

The Mythos zero-day story is being covered as a security story. It is actually an economics story. Mythos found a 17-year-old FreeBSD remote code execution vulnerability that survived millions of automated tests and decades of human review—in weeks of internal operation. The unit cost of finding a critical vulnerability just collapsed. Anthropic's controlled-release model buys defenders a head start, but that window narrows every time a comparable capability appears elsewhere. The structural question is not "how dangerous is Mythos?" but "what does software maintenance look like when every organisation—including adversarial ones—has access to a model of equivalent capability?" That question has no satisfying answer yet, and it deserves more column inches than it is getting.

*— [Anthropic Project Glasswing](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing)*
