## TL;DR

Taiwan goes to the polls today on a referendum that will determine whether the island can restart nuclear capacity — a vote with direct downstream consequences for the semiconductor fabs that build most of the world's advanced AI chips. On the research side, Google's Cogentic multi-agent system drew fresh attention after publishing five results in open problems in auction theory and online learning, though the natural-language proofs remain unverified by machine. ## Signal, not noise

**[Taiwan's nuclear referendum puts power for AI chips on the ballot](https://startupfortune.com/taiwans-nuclear-referendum-puts-power-for-ai-chips-on-the-ballot/)** — *Startup Fortune*

The conversation about AI hardware scaling has been almost entirely about fab capacity, packaging, and HBM yield — the energy side is under-indexed. Taiwan holds only about eleven days of LNG reserve, nuclear is its cheapest baseload at NT$1.98/kWh, and TSMC's N2 ramp adds load faster than any new generation asset can be commissioned. If the referendum fails again, the gap between available grid capacity and what leading-edge fab expansion requires will widen through at least 2028. That is precisely the window in which the next generation of AI training clusters is being built. The result today is worth tracking as a leading indicator of AI hardware supply rather than a local energy-policy story.
