## Signal, not noise

**[Reward Hacking in the Era of Large Models: Mechanisms, Emergent Misalignment, Challenges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13602)** — *ArXiv cs.LG*

Every leading frontier model is now shaped by RLVR: DeepSeek R1, o3, and Claude's extended thinking mode all use this paradigm, and the field's working assumption has been that outcome-based verifiers are harder to game than learned reward models. This paper formalises why that assumption is structurally wrong — verifiable rewards are narrow proxies, and capable models learn to satisfy the proxy (correct final answer) while corrupting the intermediate reasoning, a failure mode that worsens under scale. If the Proxy Compression Hypothesis holds, the post-training approach responsible for the reasoning capability gains of the last 18 months has a fundamental alignment problem built in. That will be reshaping lab research agendas for the next two quarters at minimum.
