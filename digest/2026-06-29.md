## TL;DR

The U.S. government's grip on frontier AI tightened and partially eased in the same week: the Commerce Department unblocked Anthropic's Mythos 5 for a list of 100+ approved partners while keeping Fable 5 restricted, and OpenAI's new GPT-5.6 Sol launched in limited preview to only 20 government-cleared firms. Google DeepMind absorbed its worst talent week in years, losing six named researchers — including Nobel laureate John Jumper and Transformer co-inventor Noam Shazeer — to Anthropic and OpenAI, with pre-IPO equity at rival labs cited as the structural driver. Underneath the noise, a White House executive order signed earlier this month has quietly handed the NSA authority to classify which AI models are capable of cyberwarfare, sketching the first real regulatory architecture for frontier AI without a single congressional vote.

The Anthropic export ban and the government-gated GPT-5.6 launch landed in the same 72-hour window — and that is the story. What is being assembled here, without a congressional vote, is a tiered access regime for frontier AI: the most capable models are becoming legally usable only by entities the executive branch has approved. The 100-company "trusted partners" list for Mythos 5 is a live prototype of that regime. The NSA benchmarking mandate from the June 2 EO provides the classification machinery. In three months, the question worth asking is not which model is strongest — it is who controls the list of who gets to use it.
