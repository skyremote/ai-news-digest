# AI Digest — 2026-07-02

## TL;DR

The US government's emerging practice of gating frontier model releases reached its logical conclusion this week: GPT-5.6 Sol launched under a White House-mandated preview period after crossing "High" risk thresholds in cybersecurity, biology, and chemistry simultaneously — a first for any model family. In the same 24-hour window, the administration reversed course on Anthropic, lifting export controls on Fable 5 and Mythos 5 after a three-week freeze proved operationally unworkable. Meanwhile infrastructure capital continues to consolidate: Together AI closed $800M and Meta confirmed plans to enter the cloud compute market directly.

## Signal, not noise

**[OpenAI limits GPT-5.6 rollout after government request](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/26/openai-limits-gpt-5-6-rollout-after-government-request-says-restrictions-shouldnt-be-the-norm/)** — *TechCrunch*

The coverage treats this as a launch story. It is actually a governance story. Trump's June 2 executive order created a voluntary 30-day government preview window for frontier models — and OpenAI, despite objecting to the principle, complied. The mechanism now exists, applied, and worked. The question for the next three months is whether "voluntary" holds once a model is denied clearance, whether the framework spreads to open-weight releases, and whether the EU and China read this as a template worth copying. The benchmark that triggered it — 96.7% on an offensive cybersecurity CTF — is also a calibration point every safety team at every frontier lab now has to beat before publishing.
