# AI News Digest — 2026-11-14

## Executive Summary

Jensen Huang's abrupt departure from Nvidia is the most consequential single-person exit the AI industry has seen: it simultaneously destabilises the leading GPU franchise, the open-source model ecosystem Nvidia was quietly underwriting, and the Washington lobbying operation that had held off the worst export-control proposals. Underneath that noise, OpenAI's Q3 revenue crossing $10B confirms the enterprise adoption curve is real—while a buried figure from the same reporting cycle suggests Anthropic may now be generating more quarterly revenue than OpenAI. The week's best technical result is a sparse-attention architecture that cuts the cost of million-token context to logarithmic scaling, which matters more for the next generation of reasoning agents than any headline model launch.

## Top 5 Stories

**1. Jensen Huang resigns from Nvidia; Colette Kress steps in as interim CEO**

Huang left all roles—CEO, president, board member—after a board investigation connected him to a relationship with a suspected intelligence operative. The immediate fallout is threefold: Nvidia's direct venture commitments (estimated at tens of billions to AI startups) are now in question; the Nemotron open-weight model programme he personally championed has no obvious internal heir; and the Senate Banking Committee has already signalled it will subpoena both Huang and Commerce Secretary Lutnick. AMD is the mechanical beneficiary, but no competitor has the full-stack ecosystem to absorb Nvidia's supply-chain relationships in the short run.

**2. OpenAI Q3 revenue hits $10B; Anthropic reportedly ahead at ~$17B**

Bloomberg and the Wall Street Journal confirmed OpenAI generated roughly $10B in Q3 2026, up approximately 50% from the prior quarter's $6.7B. The figure validates the enterprise-sales thesis but the more striking data point—buried in secondary reporting—is that Anthropic disclosed approximately $17B in revenue for the same period. If that holds up under scrutiny, it marks a reversal of the competitive narrative that has persisted since GPT-4's release.

*—[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/openais-quarterly-revenue-reportedly-crosses-10b-for-the-first-time/), [CryptoBriefing on Q3](https://cryptobriefing.com/openai-revenue-hits-10-billion-q3/)*

**3. Four nonprofits file suit to freeze frontier AI training**

A coalition of four nonprofits filed in California federal court seeking to enjoin training runs above an undisclosed compute threshold at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, and SpaceX. The legal theory is novel and the injunction request is aggressive; courts have been reluctant to issue pre-emptive injunctions in technology cases. Worth watching because it is the first litigation that, if granted, would directly constrain capability development rather than post-hoc liability.

*—Reported via AI Weekly aggregator, Nov 13 2026*

---

**4. Google adds Gemini 4 Hydrogen to AI Mode in Search**

Google rolled out Gemini 4 Hydrogen—a smaller, latency-optimised variant of the Gemini 4 family—into the AI Mode layer of Search. The upgrade targets multi-step queries and delivers richer visual answer cards. This is an infrastructure move as much as a product one: AI Mode query volume is now large enough that Google is deploying model-tier routing to manage cost per query.

*—[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/googles-latest-search-upgrade-promises-better-answers-to-your-most-complicated-questions/)*

---

**5. CoreWeave leans on customer prepayments as a financing instrument**

CoreWeave's Q3 earnings revealed it is structuring customer contracts with large upfront payments and shorter durations, then borrowing against those receivables. It is a creative way to fund GPU acquisitions without fresh equity, but it concentrates credit risk on a handful of enterprise contracts and shortens the revenue-visibility horizon that debt markets price against.

*—[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/coreweave-turns-to-customer-prepayments-shorter-contracts-to-fund-ai-growth/)*

## Signal, Not Noise

**Recursive Sparse Attention: million-token context at logarithmic cost** *(arXiv:2611.07232, cs.CL)*

A new attention mechanism achieves effective million-token length extrapolation with complexity that scales logarithmically rather than quadratically in sequence length, by composing sparse local windows with recursive global summaries. If the empirical results replicate on tasks where long-range reasoning actually matters—multi-document synthesis, long-horizon agent loops, full-codebase comprehension—this is a more load-bearing result than any of this week's model releases. The constraint on useful context length has been engineering cost, not architecture; a credible solution to that unlocks a generation of agent designs that are currently impractical.

*—[arXiv:2611.07232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07232)*
